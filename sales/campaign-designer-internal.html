Positioning

Lead with the loop, close on accountability.

Three sentences, in order. They work in a lift and they work in a steering committee.

1 · What it is

"You describe the outcome. It works out the strategy, builds the journey in your own platform, runs it, and comes back with a better one."

2 · Why it is different

"Everyone else optimises inside a campaign somebody already designed. This decides what the campaign should be."

3 · Why a bank can run it

"Nothing is built until your manager approves it, and every decision it made is on the record afterwards."

If the client already runs one of them

That is not an objection. Ask who decides what the campaign should be in the first place, and how long that takes. We sit upstream of their decisioning, not against it.

When they quote numbers at you

  • They will: 128% engagement, 105% cross-sells, 2x conversions.
  • Those are per-user optimisation results on consumer apps, not campaign-strategy results on a regulated card portfolio.
  • We have nothing published and we do not borrow. Answer on mechanism and accountability, then offer a pilot that produces our own number.
The demo

Four beats, ten minutes.

  1. Frame it. "Your team opens a journey builder already knowing the answer. Watch what happens when something asks first."
  2. Type the objective, then answer badly. Say "not sure" to one question on purpose. It records the gap instead of inventing a reason — this is the moment that separates us from a prompt wrapper.
  3. Read the plan aloud. Who enters, what each message does, and what it says it cannot do. This is the credibility moment; do not rush it.
  4. Confirm, then change it. Build the journey, then ask for a change in one sentence. Finish on the canvas inside their own console.

Do not say

  • "It learns from your campaigns." Not yet — that is the next stage. Say it is built to, and show the loop.
  • "It runs campaigns autonomously." Nothing is autonomous today. Approval is required for every build.
  • Any lift or conversion number. We have none published, and the product refuses invented numbers — quoting one undercuts the whole pitch.
  • "It replaces your campaign team." It replaces the blank canvas.
  • Another client's programme by name, unless marketing has cleared it.

Qualify early

  • How long does one campaign take them to get live today?
  • Do they run experiments, and can they say which arm won on the last one?
  • Is Smart Nudges already live in their tenant, or does this land with an implementation?
  • Who approves an agent touching customer comms — and are they in the room?
After the first pilot

Three numbers to collect.

We cannot match their stat bands until we have our own. These are the ones worth having, and all three are measurable from the platform itself.

Time from brief to live campaign

Measured against how long the same team takes by hand. The easiest number to win on.

Campaigns per manager per month

Throughput, before and after. Speaks to headcount without threatening it.

Lift against the control group

An agent-designed journey against the holdout the platform already runs. The only number a CFO cares about.
