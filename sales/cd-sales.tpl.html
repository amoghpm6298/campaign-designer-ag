How it works

One objective in. A campaign that keeps getting better out.

Seven stages, in the order they happen. Every screen below is from the working product.

01
Context

It starts from what you already have.

No setup. No training. No briefing document. The agent reads your channels, templates, attributes and events before it asks anything.

Everything it knows is on one page, and your team can correct it.

02
Discovery

It works out who converts, and what stops the rest.

Who already converts, what stops everyone else, and how success will be judged. The agent works this out from your data where it exists, and from your team where it does not. It keeps going until the brief is settled.

03
Strategy

It decides what the campaign should be.

The agent weighs two genuinely different approaches and picks one. It writes the full sequence of messages. The reasoning sits beside the plan, so you can argue with the thinking.

04
Approval

Nothing is built until you say so.

The whole journey is on screen before you decide. Confirm it, or say what to change in a sentence. The agent rewrites the plan and brings it back, as many times as you like. Nothing exists until you confirm.

05
Execution

It builds into the journey engine you already run.

The agent builds it through the same builder your team uses by hand. What appears is an ordinary campaign. You can edit it, pause it or kill it.

It lands in the same five-step wizard your team uses. Guardrails and review are still ahead of it, exactly as they would be by hand.

06
Measurement

Then it finds out whether it worked.

Every step carries its own numbers. Reach, delivery, opens and conversion, measured against the control group. This is not a dashboard someone has to remember to read. It is the input to what happens next.

07
Improvement

And comes back with a better version.

The agent reads what the campaign actually did. Then it proposes the next one. Drop the arm that lost. Move the message nobody read. Keep the split that worked.

The same approval step applies. It proposes, you decide. Ask for a new campaign instead and it starts from the same evidence.

With your data, or without it

It does not wait for a data project.

Most agents are useless until your data is clean and connected. This one gets to the same brief either way.

{{TWOWAY}}

When the data is there

The agent queries your customer, transaction and campaign tables for the answers it would otherwise have to ask for. Your team is only asked what the data cannot settle.

When it is not

No integration, no data project, no waiting. The agent asks what a strategist would ask, and the first campaign is built to produce the evidence the next one uses.

Either way the source is recorded. A finding from data is marked as a finding. A belief from a person stays a belief.

FAQ

Questions we get asked.

Is this a chatbot writing campaigns?

No. The agent proposes and the platform decides. A design that narrows the audience at the door, invents a number, or claims a test it cannot settle is refused before you see it.

Can it message our customers on its own?

No. The agent designs. Your team approves. Your comms stack sends, with DNC, NPA and quiet hours applied.

We have no campaign history in the platform. Does it work?

Yes. This is the point. With data connected the agent queries it. With none, it interviews your team instead and the first campaign is built to produce the evidence the next one uses. Nothing is invented to fill the gap either way.

Who is accountable for what it builds?

The manager who approved it. Every question, finding and check is kept. The decision trail can be read back at any time.

How is this different from AI decisioning in our engagement tools?

Those optimise inside a campaign someone already designed. They choose the message, the channel and the time. This agent decides what the campaign should be, then hands it to your journey engine to run.

What do you need from us to start?

A tenant with your channels and templates configured. Reading your customer and campaign data is the next stage. That needs its own conversation.
